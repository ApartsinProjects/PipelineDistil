## Slide 1 — Unsupervised knowledge distillation for multiple Blackbox ML pipelines

Title slide, decorative only. A dark-blue watercolor-splash background carries the deck title in white, no author, date, or affiliation line. Sets the scope in one phrase: **unsupervised** (no labels available for the student), **multiple** (several teacher pipelines, not one), **Blackbox** (teachers are opaque, only inputs and outputs are visible), and **ML pipelines** (not just neural networks — full pre/post processing chains).

## Slide 2 — Brief and Goals

Definition-plus-plan slide split into two zones. The upper half is a three-bullet definition block: (1) **KD** is defined as compressing a large teacher into a smaller student "without loss of validity" so the student can run on edge devices, (2) KD generalizes to **several teacher models that share certain inputs** (the example given is dependent subsystems in a car), and (3) in the **anomaly-detection setting** the student has to detect anomalies under conditions absent from training, which the slide labels as **covariate shift** and links to two remedies, **synthetic anomaly generation (SAG)** and **gap filling (GF)**. Three phrases are yellow-highlighted as the load-bearing vocabulary: "Knowledge distillation (KD)", "covariate shift", and "synthetic anomaly generation (SAG) or gap filling (GF)". The bottom-left of the slide carries a four-step process diagram — Step 1 (Pipeline): train a big teacher via pipeline execution; Step 2 (SAG): generate synthetic data for unseen anomalies; Step 3 (KD): use the teacher model and synthetic data to train the student; Step 4 (Validation): compare student vs teacher accuracy. The bottom-right shows a t-SNE-style "SAG example" scatter over the range roughly x ∈ [-100, 100], y ∈ [-60, 60], with three colored classes — blue "Synthetic anomaly" points spread over most of the plane, orange "Normal data" concentrated in the upper-right cluster, and a smaller green "Known anomaly" cluster on the left — visualizing how SAG fills the region of feature space between and around the known modes.

## Slide 3 — Implementation

Two-block architecture slide showing how SAG and KD are wired as trainable computation graphs, with the loss functions written out on the right. The upper block ("SAG — Synthetic anomaly generation") is a left-to-right dataflow diagram: **Input signals X** and **Teacher output y** both feed into an **Estimation of probability density p(x), p(y)** stage, whose gradients `grad(x), grad(y)` and the raw signals feed a **Generative FC neural network** that emits **Generated signals X_g** and **Generated teacher output y_g**; a **Loss computation** block then backpropagates through both the generator and the density estimator (two labeled `backpropagate` arrows loop back). The lower block ("KD — knowledge distillation") is simpler: **Teacher input X** goes through an **LSTM neural network** producing **Generated output Y_g**, which is compared against **Teacher output Y_t** in a Loss-computation block that backpropagates to the LSTM. To the right, a 3-D plot titled "Probability density surface p(y) (2D case)" shows a bumpy magenta-to-cyan surface with two visible peaks and a saddle over x, y ∈ [-15, 15], illustrating what the density estimator learns and why the SAG loss uses its gradient. Two yellow-highlighted loss formulas anchor the method: `L_SAG = (y_g - T(x_g)) + p^x(x_g) + p^y(y_g) - cos(x - x_g, grad(p^x(x))) - cos(y - y_g, grad(p^y(y)))`, where T is the teacher, and `L_KD = (y_g - y_t)^2`. The SAG loss reads as "generated samples should disagree with the teacher, sit in low-density regions, and move *against* the density gradient" — that is, seek out the covariate-shift gaps.

## Slide 4 — Results

Two-column, two-row grid of Seaborn-style histograms comparing the score distributions of the student model in two training regimes. The left column is labeled **Student** (highlighted yellow) and shows plain KD; the right column is **KD with SAG** (highlighted yellow) and shows KD trained on the SAG-augmented data. Each column carries the same two panels: on top, **Teacher health scores (test data)** — a blue "normal" mode centered around 7–8 and an orange "abnormal" mode centered around 15–16 with a small overlap around 10–13, essentially the reference target both students should reproduce. On the bottom, **Student health scores (test data)**: the plain student (left) squashes the two classes together — normal centered near 8 and abnormal shifted down to about 12, with substantial overlap in the 10–13 band. The SAG-augmented student (right) recovers a much cleaner separation — normal mode centered near 7 with a lower shoulder at 6, abnormal mode sharp and tall at ~12, with the two distributions barely overlapping. The pedagogical takeaway is visual: **SAG does not make the student match the teacher's absolute score range, but it restores the bimodality and the class separability that plain KD collapses.**

## Slide 5 — Edge optimization and model fusion

Closing slide that pivots from "does it work statistically" to "how does it ship." A four-bullet text block explains the deployment stack: (1) the distilled model targets an **edge device (vehicle)** and uses a framework-agnostic inference runtime, specifically **ONNX** and **TVM**; (2) models from **PyTorch, TensorFlow, and Caffe** convert into the unified computation-graph format whose parameters are emitted as **pure C or C++**, removing the Python-interpreter dependency; (3) optional optimizations such as **parameter quantization** are applied during conversion; (4) the current toolchain path is **PyTorch → ONNX → TVM** because the ONNX converter has the richer operator coverage. Under the text sits the four-step fusion pipeline as a chained-boxes diagram: **Step 1 (N teachers training)** — train multiple black-box models; **Step 2 (One student KD)** — knowledge distillation with synthetic data (SAG output feeds here); **Step 3 (ONNX model fusion)** — convert the fused student to ONNX; **Step 4 (TVM conversion)** — convert ONNX to TVM for the target ECU. This is the exact merge-then-optimize flow that the patent later formalizes as blocks 320-360 in Fig. 3, with "N teachers → 1 student" being the concrete instantiation of "multiple heterogeneous MLIPs → one target model."

## Deck-level takeaway

The deck is the four-slide engineering pitch for what became patent US20240013095A1: **many independently-trained blackbox pipelines are fused into one edge-deployable student via unsupervised knowledge distillation**, with **synthetic anomaly generation (SAG)** providing the training coverage that pure output-matching KD cannot, and **ONNX/TVM** providing the deployment path onto an in-vehicle ECU. Its pedagogical signature is "one big idea per slide, always paired with an operational artifact": the goal slide (2) pairs the KD definition with a 4-step process diagram and a real SAG scatter; the implementation slide (3) pairs the two dataflow graphs with their exact loss expressions and a density-surface visualization; the results slide (4) pairs the claim ("SAG helps") with a direct before/after histogram comparison; the deployment slide (5) pairs the ONNX/TVM rationale with the concrete N-teachers → 1-student pipeline. Read alongside the patent, the deck is the "why and how" (SAG as the covariate-shift fix, LSTM student, ONNX/TVM as the runtime) that sits behind the patent's more abstract "obtain pipelines → merge → optimize → target model" claim language.
